What is a Cyclorama?

A Cyclorama is a very large 360-degree oil painting with various additional props and effects to create the illusion of immersion into a grand scene, such as a battle or rebellion.

They were often 50ft/15m high with 400ft/120m perimeters. So large were these artworks that they had to be housed in custom constructed buildings. The Final Act takes place within such an exhibit, as a painting of the 1854 Eureka Rebellion comes alive!

Investigators are initially approached by the newly formed occult branch of the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR), the forerunner to modern-day CSIRO. They are asked to investigate strange phenomena associated with a photograph of a marble bust of Sir Redmond Barry, the judge who presided over much of the Eureka trials, with a view to commercialising any discoveries “in the National Interest”.

The original bust is somewhere within the grand cultural complex housing the State Library, Museum and National Gallery — the setting for this adventure!

The mysterious photo is a stereograph: two images taken side by side and viewed through a stereoscope to create the illusion of a 3D scene. Through the course of the investigation, other haunted stereographs are uncovered, as well as a haunted painting in the galleries. Each involves either the Eureka Rebellion, Cycloramas or both.

This culminates in the discovery of the source of the haunting (a surviving portion of the Eureka Cyclorama painting, hidden in storage in the basement). Investigators are transported to the Cyclorama of old inside a pocket universe. Will investigators survive the painting as it comes alive? Will they be able to escape?

Find out as you play TO THE CYCLORAMA!
