Getting Started

Provide players Handout A Melbourne. Investigators find themselves in the hustle and bustle of Melbourne, outside the Library, Museum & Gallery shown at the top of the handout. The handout also holds the Russell Street photo (a clue on p. 9) and Sam’s parcel: three stereographs and a stereoscope.

Investigators may be CSIR scientists, members of its secret occult branch, or consultants hired for the job.

The Busker (Optional)

This incredibly optional musical number is thematic, entirely free of clues and goes to the character and ultimate fate of Mr Cy Roe. Poetry about Australian Prime Ministers and the founding of the CSIR is very niche, but some tables love it! Handout B The Busker’s Ballad.

A new CSIR recruit, Sam is delivering three stereographs and a stereoscope to investigators. Sam understands the CSIR was formed because:

“Science is too important to be left to scientists. Science must serve the National Interest. It must be guided by businessmen and industry!”

“Moreover, the occult is too important to be left to the occultists!”

A secret CSIR branch has been formed, and its first job is to investigate and commercialise this cursed (or worse) photograph.

The Stereograph

Sam explains that a stereograph is two photos taken from slightly different angles. Through the stereoscope they become one 3D scene.

The strange one (next page) features a marble bust of Sir Redmond Barry, which is located somewhere inside. The other two, of the statues out front, seem ordinary (p. 6).

Sir Redmond Barry

Visionary. Jurist. Patron. Formidable.

Founder of the University and the Public Library, Sir Redmond Barry sat on the bench for some thirty years. He tried the Eureka rebels, those goldfields diggers who stood behind a ramshackle stockade, protesting exorbitant licences. A Melbourne jury set every one of them free.

Ned Kelly he sent to the gallows.
