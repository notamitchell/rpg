The Barry Stereograph

Sir Redmond Barry bust, stereograph by B. C. Boake.

Sam won’t look

Sam hands over the stereoscope and stereographs, but keeps well back.

“The St. George and Joan of Arc photos are fine. They were just found next to the other one, on a desk in the library inside. I’ve already looked at the Barry one once. I’m not looking again, but you are…”

“Like I said, the creepy Barry bust is somewhere inside. St. George, Joan and the lion are right behind you, next to the steps. I’m not worried about them.”

The spirits can possess the photographed bust, not the bust itself, which is entirely mundane (see Latrobe Gallery). Like the other haunted stereographs, the spirit within the card is trying to draw attention to the Eureka Cyclorama painting in the basement through choice of topic (Eureka judge) and mouthing words. Once Sam has gone, the Joan of Arc card in the parcel is possessed and changes (p. 6).

Through the Stereoscope

The phenomenon only appears through the stereoscope, to one viewer at a time. Each viewing picks up where the last one left off.

First viewing. Two images swim together and synchronise into one. Then the marble eyes float downwards, settling towards the floor.

Second viewing. It looks down, as if it always had. Yet the head still looks up, projecting pride. The lips part, but no teeth can be found behind. It tries to speak, but no sound comes.

Spot

The clumsy stone lips, worked by an amateur, are shaping one word. Pillow? Millow? Billow? Fellow? Below!

Third viewing. The bust strains towards the viewer. Still looking down, it mouths a new word, slower this time.

Spot

Town? Noun? Down! The word is down!

Further viewings. Nothing. A bust, a wall, the grain of the photograph. But the eyes stay where they were left: looking down.
