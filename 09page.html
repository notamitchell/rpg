Research in the Library

Stereographs Research

The library holds thousands of stereographs, some of them photos of the library, gallery and museum themselves. To find a specific card, investigators need its catalogue name or a unique keyword (for a gallery statue, its plaque name). Eliza can provide boxes on boxes, with keyword-based index cards.

There are two main paths for investigators. Browsing a box can turn up the Russell Street card, noticed by its faint, soft supernatural glow. Alternatively, the haunted portrait of rebel leader Peter Lalor in the McArthur Gallery (p. 17) might prompt research into the Eureka Rebellion. Both paths can lead to the topic of Cycloramas and in particular the basement Eureka canvas.

Siege of Paris

943.082 SIEDescribeKnowledge (History)

Paris, Siege of, 1870–1871

Prussian armies ringed Paris from September 1870 to January 1871. Cut off, the city sent its letters out by balloon and ate its horses, its rats and at last the zoo’s elephants before it surrendered.

Robert W. Chambers set “The Street of the First Shell”, a story in his 1895 book The King in Yellow, during the siege.

Cyclorama, Siege of Paris

The sign on the building was for something called “The Cyclorama of the Siege of Paris”. Since exhibition attendees already knew what a Cyclorama was, the booklet never fully explains their nature. Its best hints are the fold-out ring diagram and a 50 ft × 400 ft canvas stretched round a circular building.
