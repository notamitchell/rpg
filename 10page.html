Eureka Rebellion, 1854

The library holds a great many works on the Eureka Rebellion, a landmark of Australian history. Gold was found in Victoria in 1851, and people came from across the world to dig at Ballarat. They paid a monthly licence but had no right to vote. Armed police, foot and mounted (the “troopers”), hunted anyone without one. By 1854 the diggers had had enough of authority overreach and began open rebellion.

Eureka Stockade, 1854 (general works)

In late 1854 the diggers raised a stockade among their tents, little more than a fence. At dawn on Sunday 3 December redcoats, cavalry, foot police and troopers attacked. The fight was short and decisive: the pikemen broke, the troopers rode them down, and a foot policeman tore down the diggers’ Southern Cross flag.
