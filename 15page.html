Greek Heroes

Perseus

Slayer of the Gorgon

Son of Zeus and the mortal Danaë, Perseus cut off the head of the snake-haired Gorgon Medusa, whose gaze turned men to stone.

With winged sandals, a cap of darkness and a polished shield, he never looked at her directly, only at her reflection.

Even severed, the head kept its power. He turned it on his enemies and left them standing, white and still, for ever.

Perseus the statue

Spot

The base of the statue is modern and has a metal door with a keyhole.

Fine Manipulation

The door opens, revealing a series of switches. One is separate from all of the others, coloured red and labelled ALARM.

These are light switches for all of the galleries; their strange placement is an artefact of electrical retrofitting.

Investigators who open it are likely to be caught at it by the security guard and given a formal warning. The suspicious Security Guard River then patrols near Eliza’s podium (p. 8), making it harder for investigators to steal a key from there or from their belt.
