Latrobe Gallery

Young bull and herdsman, stereograph by B. C. Boake.

This room contains the Sir Redmond Barry marble bust, the subject of the haunted stereograph. It is located near a different statue whose plaque reads “Young bull and herdsman” (see above). The adjoining McAllan Gallery is closed for renovations.

The plaque under the bust explains Sir Redmond’s life in some detail:

Sir Redmond Barry 1813 – 1880 “Open to all over fourteen years of age, with clean hands. No dress jacket required.” Founder of the free Melbourne Public Library, a novel idea at the time. First Chancellor of the University of Melbourne. Judge of the Supreme Court Presided over the Eureka rebels’ trials for high treason. Sentenced Ned Kelly to death.

Playing the Anticlimax

There is nothing magical or occult about the Sir Redmond Barry marble bust itself. The spirits can work through any illusion: the Cyclorama, stereographs, the Triscenorama portrait, but not a statue.

Players have watched this bust move. Build up the approach: the long gallery, the quiet, the bull and herdsman.

Then describe the bust exactly as it appears on the card: white marble, head turned, eyes fixed straight ahead. It does not look down. It does not move.

The use of the “Young bull and herdsman” statue here is deliberate thematically. From the busker’s ballad (“Oh P.M. Brucey, Brucey, you’ve put science on a leash”), through the Circe statue and her predisposition for turning people into beasts, this all builds to the potential fate of Mr Roe in the Final Act.
