Peter Lalor

Rebel & Speaker

He led the diggers at the Eureka Stockade, where they knelt:

“We swear by the Southern Cross to stand truly by each other and fight to defend our rights and liberties.”

Shot in the left arm in the dawn attack of 3 December 1854, he lost the arm to the surgeon but never fell to the troopers: the £200 on his head went unclaimed. Once the rebels were acquitted, Ballarat sent him to Parliament.

This room has only one statue, Maternal Affection; the rest is paintings on the wall and rows of wooden chairs for patrons to sit and ponder the great works. One painting stands out, literally.

Three Views of Peter Lalor: the Triscenorama

From the left: before the fight

Lalor administers the oath to the diggers under the Southern Cross flag, as hundreds kneel, hats in hand.

From the front: after Eureka

Gaunt and hunted, the left sleeve empty and pinned. A fugitive with a price on his head. Shot in the dawn attack, he hid under a pile of slabs. The arm came off next day.

From the right: much later

The Honourable Peter Lalor, grey-bearded Speaker of the people’s Legislative Assembly, in the Speaker’s robes and wig.
