A Dead Body

No roll

Dental forceps in the right pocket. Three gold teeth in the left pocket, roots and all. A New Zealand Alpine Club card. Staff ID: Charlie.

Medicine

Dead within minutes: bled out from the dagger still in their stomach. It went in upwards, from below (Gamemaster: Charlie was climbing, and the horseman reached up).

Appraise/
Knowledge (History)

That’s not a new dagger. It’s an old dagger from the gold-digging days, the exact twin of the one on the table, down to the nicks in the blade.

Medicine or Appraise

Gold-foil fillings: 1850s dentistry. Each is exactly the same as nine teeth in the pouch: same filling, same chip, same wear. The forceps scratches differ.

Science (Geology)

Boots caked in Ballarat clay with chips of quartz, likely from the Eureka lead.

Stereographs on the Body

No roll

A bundle of stereograph cards, every one painted over.

Research

Library stamps on the back: these are all cards missing from the stereograph boxes. All relate to Eureka, gold mining, Ballarat or the Cyclorama.

The Canvas

Research

Paperwork indicates the archivist Charlie has been working with the canvas a lot.

Spot

A bloody handprint on the canvas edge, still wet. It looks like the hand has gripped the painting somehow.

Idea or Knowledge (Occult)

The canvas isn’t haunted, it’s a prison. Whatever is bound to it only wakes when someone crosses in.

Touching the Canvas

Anyone whose skin touches the painted surface crosses in, and takes everyone else in the storeroom with them. The painting slowly changes in response to activity within the pocket universe. It won’t burn: the paint never dries. Only returning what was stolen rests the dead.

The paint is warm, and gives like skin. Then the table is gone, and the basement, and the flickering bulb. Wind. You are standing on a wooden platform, high up, in the middle of a battlefield?
