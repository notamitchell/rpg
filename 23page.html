Inside the Cyclorama of Yesteryear

Arrival. Investigators arrive atop the viewing platform, in the middle of the Cyclorama exhibit as it once stood: half-painted and empty of visitors.

A colossal canvas borders the room. The canvas stretches perhaps fifty feet (15 m) high and a staggering four hundred feet (120 m) around the perimeter. It attaches to the ceiling by a complex rigging reminiscent of the sails of a massive galleon. It’s taut, yet not rigidly cylindrical. Instead, it curves with a sinuous grace, cinched towards its centre at the very base and flaring outwards towards the top like a colossal trumpet.

Between you and the paint, below the viewing platform, is fifty feet (15 m) of real ground: rocks, bushes, fake turf, pools of water. Wax and straw men posed frozen among them, mid-charge, mid-flight and mid-blow. A trooper sits a stuffed horse. Others lie dead and wounded beside broken weapons. A real wagon with a broken wheel, its wax fruit spilled in the fake mud. Then the ground becomes paint, and the eye cannot find the join.

Above, a huge painted canopy, an umbrella of sky, with a few faint seagulls in its furls. Beyond its edge, grimy skylights let in the only real light in this place. (Spot: the light flickers, just like the bulb in the basement.)

Points of interest

  • The platform. An outer railing rings its edge. An inner railing guards a spiral staircase down through the pillar that holds it up. The platform is unfinished: from the stairs near ground level, investigators can jump down onto the faux terrain.
  • The tunnel. Down the spiral stairs (see p. 25).
  • The unfinished half. In places the paint simply gives out: raw canvas, pencil underdrawing, soldiers who are nothing but outlines, a stretch of sky where the painter ran out of blue. The finished half is mostly the painting from the basement, extended further round the stockade.
  • The railway. A narrow track runs close to the canvas, carrying staging carriages of different heights. Every level is cluttered with paint pots and brushes. The tallest reaches almost to the beams beneath the skylights (p. 26).

The Calm Before

When the last person is in, the scene holds its breath. Before long the bugle sounds and the battle begins (p. 24). Each new arrival, or the end of a battle, resets the scene, and the calm begins again.

There is time to look around.
