Appendix Basic Roleplaying System

To the Cyclorama is written for Basic Roleplaying (BRP), Chaosium’s percentile system.

QuickstartWe don’t recommend To the Cyclorama as your first ever roleplaying game. Chaosium’s free introduction to BRP includes three sample adventures geared towards beginners. But everything you need to play To the Cyclorama is explained in the linked free PDF. We’ve included a Quick Reference for you below that is specific to this game.

Before the Final Act

Most of BRP, and everything before the finale of To the Cyclorama, is skill rolls: two ten-sided dice, one for tens and one for ones, for a total from 01 to 100.

BRP is a bit like golf: rolling lower is better. If a character sheet lists Hide at 10, that investigator succeeds 10% of the time, on any roll ≤ 10.

Example: lip reading (p. 5). To read a stone bust’s silent lips, the gamemaster calls for a Spot roll. The player’s Spot is 50. They roll 30 and 03, for a total of 33: a success, and the gamemaster tells them the word is below. A roll of 80 and 02 makes 82, higher than 50, so it fails and no lips are read.

Where the text offers two skills, such as Knowledge (History)/Idea, use whichever makes more sense or is higher. No skill fits? Roll a characteristic × 5: Idea (INT) for a flash of insight, Luck (POW) for pure chance.

Key skills: Research and Spot find the clues, Knowledge (History) and (Occult) explain them, Fine Manipulation opens the staff door, and Climb and Jump get everyone out.

The Final Act

The Final Act is mostly fighting and fleeing, in 12-second rounds. Each player acts once a round; highest DEX goes first.

Attacks are skill rolls too. When someone swings at you, you can parry with a weapon or Dodge, but Dodging costs you your own attack that round. Nobody can Dodge a bullet: get behind cover, which halves a shooter’s chance. An attack from behind doubles it.

Example: The trooper by the wagon swings at Terry, Carbine butt 40%, and rolls 23: a hit. Terry Dodges at 45% and rolls 61: a miss. Damage is 1D6+DM; the trooper has no DM, so a roll of 5 makes 5 damage.

At 1–2 HP you’re unconscious; at 0 you die unless First Aid (1D3 HP) saves you that round.

Wrestling something out of someone’s hands is a resistance roll. Start at 50%: add 5% per point your STR beats theirs, take off 5% per point it falls short. An investigator with STR 10, wrestling the sword from the redcoat officer (STR 12), wins on 40 or under.
