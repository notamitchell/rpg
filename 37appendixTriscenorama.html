Appendix Triscenorama

A Triscenorama is an optical illusion art piece created using a specially corrugated or accordion-folded surface. Depending on the angle from which it is viewed, completely different images are revealed:

Instead of traditional angled corrugations, a Triscenorama features fronds that extend straight out from the portrait at a 90-degree angle. As a result, viewers can see a third distinct image from the front:

A Triscenorama can be made by folding a flat image like the one shown at the bottom of this page. The floppy accordion-like structure can be reinforced by glueing it to a cardboard backing. Such a setup allows you to:

  1. Show players the view from the right.
  2. Show players the front view.
  3. Show players the left view while you surreptitiously poke the eyes out of the front-view portrait. You can do this with a pencil from behind the canvas.
  4. Show players the front view again with the eyes missing.

Before the game, print and fold Appendix Triscenorama Portrait (p. 38): a three-way portrait of Peter Lalor. Show it to players from the left, front and right, but keep it in your hands.
