The Eureka Stockade

Posts were erected, with saplings, and palings and slabs fastened between them, to make a kind of fence, and thus the Eureka Stockade was formed.

This was a flimsy, useless construction altogether, without the slightest pretension to military engineering …

The insurgents were armed with double and single-barrelled guns, rifles, revolvers, pistols, pikes, and pitchforks, and a German blacksmith worked incessantly in a small forge near the waterhole making pikes for such as had no firearms.

Mr. Lalor posted sentries round the Stockade, and gave “Vinegar Hill” as the night password; but neither he nor his adherents ever anticipated an attack.

… It was just as the radiance of the rising sun began to show over Mt. Warrenheip that the warning shot was fired; when the orb of day was a handbreadth above the horizon all was over.

The Picture

… This picture is by far the largest work of pictorial art that has ever been produced in Australia, covering, as it does, about 00,000 square feet of canvas, and has been supplied by Messrs. Reed and Gross, the celebrated Cycloramists.

The moment chosen for the scene of the picture is when the affray was at its height, after the first volley, and when some of the military skirmishers had forced their way inside, at the instant when the bugle sounded for the main body of troops to advance from the gully behind the Free Trade Hotel, where the American flag is seen flying.

To the left a determined stand is being made by a handful of diggers against the advanced skirmishers on that side, while on the right is shown one of the soldiery who had already got inside and taken part in the burning of Diamond’s store, which is seen in flames, and having thrown down his musket, is taking refuge in flight from the defenders of the place.
